Pre & Post Season

Wrestling seasons are won in the months nobody is watching. Here is what the Mustang year looks like outside of November through February.

Preseason

Late summer through the first official practice.

Preseason is about arriving in shape and cleared to compete, so the first week of practice is spent wrestling instead of catching up. Everything here is open to new wrestlers — you do not need experience to start showing up.

  • June – August

    Summer lifting & camps

    Team lifting in the Mountain Crest weight room plus optional team camps and individual technique camps. This is where new wrestlers learn stance, motion and the basic scoring positions before the pace picks up.

  • September – October

    Open mats & conditioning

    Two to three open mats a week: live wrestling, drilling and conditioning. Build your base now so November is technique work, not survival.

  • October

    Paperwork & parent meeting

    Sports physical, district registration, concussion and participation forms, fees, and the preseason parent/athlete meeting. Nothing on this list can be skipped — no clearance, no mat time.

  • Late October

    First official practice

    UHSAA start date. Two weeks of acclimatization, weight certification and skin checks before the first competition.

Preseason checklist

  • Current sports physical on file
  • Online registration and district forms complete
  • Participation fee paid or a fee waiver submitted
  • Wrestling shoes, headgear and a mouthguard if you wear a retainer or braces
  • Joined the team's sportsYou channel
  • Academically eligible under UHSAA and district rules

Post Season

From the state tournament through the spring.

The high school season ends in February. The wrestlers who make the biggest jumps are the ones who keep competing in the spring — that is when technique actually changes.

  • February

    Region & state championships

    Region seeds you for the UHSAA state tournament. Everyone travels with the team — qualifiers wrestle, everyone else corners, warms up partners and cheers.

  • February

    Awards banquet & exit meetings

    Team awards, senior recognition, and one-on-one meetings with the coaches: what went well, what to work on, and a written plan for your offseason.

  • March – May

    Freestyle & folkstyle season

    Spring is the best development season there is. Freestyle rewards attacking and scoring in exchanges, and the tournaments are lower pressure. Coaches will point you to local clubs and state series events.

  • March – May

    Offseason strength

    Two to three lifts a week to build the strength base you cannot build in season. Returning wrestlers, incoming freshmen and other-sport athletes are all welcome.

  • Summer

    Camps, national events & recruiting

    Team camp, technique camps, and for wrestlers chasing college wrestling, the summer events that put you in front of coaches. Girls wrestling is one of the fastest-growing college sports — if you want to wrestle at the next level, start these conversations junior year.

Play another sport

We want multi-sport athletes. Track, soccer, softball, rugby and lacrosse all build things wrestling needs. Tell your coaches your other-sport schedule and we will build an offseason plan that fits around it.
